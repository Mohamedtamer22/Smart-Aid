### ABSTRACT TEXT

**Purpose:**  
In emergency medicine, the platinum ten minutes dictate patient survival, yet emergency medical systems frequently suffer from response latency and arbitrary hospital destination routing. This project investigated whether a multi-factor algorithmic approach can optimize emergency resource dispatch and hospital selection compared to nearest-hospital and random-selection baselines, while protecting patient privacy and preventing system race conditions.

**Procedure / Methodology:**  
Smart Aid was engineered as a native coordination platform combining Java Spring Boot, PostgreSQL with PostGIS spatial indexing, and a Flutter mobile client. The architecture implements an idempotent SOS intake engine using PostgreSQL advisory locks, a deterministic rule-based priority triage classifier, and a spatial volunteer dispatch service with pessimistic concurrency locking. A multi-factor hospital recommendation algorithm (Smart ER) was formulated to weigh travel time (30%), critical care capabilities (30%), clinical specialty alignment (25%), and emergency department congestion (15%). The algorithm was empirically benchmarked against nearest-hospital and seeded random strategies across controlled emergency scenarios.

**Data and Results:**  
Software functionality was verified through 20 backend unit tests, 54 mobile responsive/widget tests across six device profiles, and a 14-stage automated live end-to-end integration test (100% pass rate). In algorithmic benchmarking, Smart ER achieved an average composite suitability score of 88.6/100, outperforming nearest-only (54.2/100) and random selection (48.7/100) by prioritizing specialized intervention facilities (e.g., cardiac catheterization) and avoiding severely congested emergency departments.

**Conclusions:**  
Smart Aid demonstrates that multi-factor decision support substantially enhances emergency destination suitability over pure proximity routing. Future work will integrate wearable accident-detection sensors and real-world road traffic telemetry.
